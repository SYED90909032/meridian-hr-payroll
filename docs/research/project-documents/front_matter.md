# HR and Payroll Platforms: Research Dossier on Keka and 30 Alternatives

**Author:** Manus AI. **Research date:** November 2026. **Scope:** Keka as the baseline plus 30 alternatives. The dossier catalogues {TOTAL} features in total. Every figure is attributed to its source in the platform sections below.

## How the 30 platforms were chosen and ranked

The 50 alternatives from the first list were scored on four questions. The first was scale: how many organizations and employees the platform serves today. The second was breadth: how many of 34 standard HR and payroll modules it offers natively. The third was relevance to a Keka-type buyer, meaning payroll-led HR for India, the Gulf, Southeast Asia and growing global teams. The fourth was where the market is heading, judged by AI agents, MCP servers, open APIs and global payroll.

The top 20 are the platforms that shape what buyers expect worldwide. The next 10 are regional specialists. Most of them compete with Keka directly in India, the Philippines and the Middle East. Their statutory depth is the hardest part of any alternative to reproduce. Freshteam was left out because Freshworks has discontinued it.

The tiers group platforms by the job they do. They are not quality scores.

**Tier 1, global enterprise suites (ranks 1 to 6):** Workday HCM, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle Fusion Cloud HCM, UKG Pro, Dayforce and ADP Workforce Now. These have the deepest data models, very large customer bases and the most complex implementations. Their public API contracts were studied in the backend teardowns.

**Tier 2, modern unified and global-employment platforms (ranks 7 to 20):** Rippling, Deel, Darwinbox, Zoho People, BambooHR, Gusto, HiBob, Personio, Paycom, Paylocity, greytHR, Remote, Factorial and Employment Hero. This tier sets the bar for user experience, unified employee data, global payroll, EOR and, increasingly, MCP-based AI access.

**Tier 3, regional payroll and compliance specialists (ranks 21 to 30):** Paychex Flex, HROne, PeopleStrong, factoHR, Zimyo, ZingHR, Razorpay Payroll, Sprout Solutions, Bayzat and ZenHR. These platforms compete on statutory depth, such as India PF/ESI/PT/TDS, UAE WPS, Saudi GOSI/Mudad and Philippine BIR/SSS. They also compete on frontline attendance and price.

## What the coverage data shows

Each platform was classified for 34 modules: native, add-on or partial, via partner, absent, or unknown. The heatmap shows the result. A dark cell means the vendor delivers that capability itself in its core product.

![Module coverage heatmap across Keka and 30 alternatives](/home/ubuntu/hr_research/diagrams/heatmap.png)

![How many of the 31 platforms offer each module natively](/home/ubuntu/hr_research/diagrams/prevalence.png)

Some modules are table stakes, and no new platform can win on them. Core HR records and employee self-service are native in all 31 platforms. Onboarding and offboarding and mobile apps are native in 30. Domestic payroll and leave are native in 28. Org charts, analytics and an AI assistant are native in 27. Any alternative must ship these on day one. Shipping them is the minimum, not a way to stand out.

The gaps are in the right-hand columns. A native MCP server is offered by only 11 platforms and is absent or undocumented in 17. True multi-country payroll is native in only 11, and EOR and contractor management in only 4. Timesheets and project costing are native in just 6 and are usually sold as add-ons. Native HR helpdesks appear in 12. Earned wage access is native in 3 and IT device management in 2. Many vendors also split talent modules into paid tiers: compensation planning is an add-on in 18 platforms and workforce planning in 15.

The pattern matters more than any single column. Most vendors grow by bolting on modules, so buyers pay for overlapping add-ons and partner products. The blueprint responds to this. It ships one shared core that every module reuses, instead of 34 separate products.

The rest of this dossier holds one detailed profile per platform, each with a complete feature list. It then gives evidence-graded backend teardowns of the top three platforms and five cross-cutting studies. The studies cover user pain points, regulation, AI and MCP, architecture, and the open-source landscape.
